# Parent Guide — Healthy Growth for Ages 2–6

> ⚠️ **Disclaimer:** Everything below is **general, approximate guidance** based on
> WHO/IAP growth standards and CDC developmental milestones. **Every child is different and
> grows at their own pace.** This is **not medical advice** — please use your pediatrician's
> growth charts and advice for your specific child. If you have concerns about growth,
> development, eating, or behavior, talk to your doctor.

This guide is also available inside the app: **Parent Zone (⚙️) → Growth & Health Guide**.

---

## 1. Typical weight & height (ages 2–6)

Approximate healthy bands (roughly the 3rd–97th percentile midrange). Your pediatrician
tracks your child's own curve over time, which matters more than any single number.

| Age | 👧 Girl weight | 👧 Girl height | 👦 Boy weight | 👦 Boy height |
|-----|----------------|----------------|---------------|---------------|
| 2 years | 10–13.5 kg | 82–90 cm | 10.5–14 kg | 83–92 cm |
| 3 years | 11.5–16 kg | 90–99 cm | 12–16.5 kg | 91–100 cm |
| 4 years | 13–18.5 kg | 96–107 cm | 13.5–19 kg | 97–107 cm |
| 5 years | 14.5–21 kg | 102–114 cm | 15–21.5 kg | 103–114 cm |
| 6 years | 16–24 kg | 108–121 cm | 16.5–24 kg | 109–121 cm |

**What matters most:** a steady curve. A child who is consistently on the smaller or larger
side but growing steadily is usually fine. Sudden flattening or jumps are worth a check-up.

## 2. Developmental milestones

Milestones are *ranges*, not deadlines. Most children reach these around the listed age.

### 2 years 🚼
- **Movement:** runs, kicks a ball, walks up stairs holding on, stacks 4+ blocks.
- **Language:** 2-word phrases ("more milk"), points to things in a book, follows 2-step instructions.
- **Social:** copies others, excited around other kids, shows some defiance (normal!).
- **Thinking:** sorts shapes/colors, simple puzzles, begins pretend play.

### 3 years 🧒
- **Movement:** pedals a tricycle, climbs and runs well, draws lines and circles.
- **Language:** 3-word sentences, says name and age, strangers understand most speech.
- **Social:** takes turns (with help), affection for friends, make-believe play.
- **Thinking:** works toys with buttons/levers, names some colors, understands same/different.

### 4 years 👧
- **Movement:** hops and stands on one foot, catches a bounced ball, pours/cuts with help.
- **Language:** tells short stories, knows rhymes/songs, speaks in full sentences.
- **Social:** plays cooperatively, talks about interests, prefers playing with kids.
- **Thinking:** names some colors/numbers, draws a person with 2–4 parts, understands counting.

### 5 years 🧑
- **Movement:** skips and hops, uses utensils, can do a somersault.
- **Language:** speaks very clearly, tells longer stories, uses future tense.
- **Social:** wants to please friends, follows game rules, more independent.
- **Thinking:** counts to 10+, draws a person with 6 parts, prints some letters/numbers.

### 6 years 🎒
- **Movement:** rides a bicycle, learning to tie shoes, better coordination.
- **Language:** reads simple words, learning to tell time, holds a real conversation.
- **Social:** understands sharing/fairness, forms friendships, wants to be liked.
- **Thinking:** longer attention span, begins simple addition, understands left/right.

> If your child has lost skills they had, or is far behind several milestones, mention it to
> your pediatrician early — early support helps a lot.

## 3. Eating ideas (India-focused)

Aim for a balanced plate: a grain + a protein + a vegetable/fruit + dairy, with mild spice
and soft textures for the youngest.

### 🌅 Breakfast
- Idli with mild sambar or coconut chutney
- Soft dosa / uttapam with veggies
- Poha with peas and a little lemon
- Vegetable upma or rava porridge
- Moong dal or besan chilla (mini pancakes)
- Ragi porridge or ragi malt with jaggery
- Paratha (aloo / methi / paneer) with curd
- Oats or daliya cooked in milk with banana
- Egg bhurji or boiled egg with toast

### 🍛 Lunch & dinner
- Dal–rice with a spoon of ghee
- Soft roti with sabzi (lauki, palak, carrot, beans)
- Vegetable khichdi (moong dal + rice + veg)
- Curd rice with grated carrot
- Rajma or chole (mild) with rice
- Vegetable pulao with raita
- Sambar rice or rasam rice
- Paneer bhurji or palak paneer (mild)
- Mild fish or chicken curry with rice (if non-veg)

## 4. Healthy snack ideas 🍎

- Fresh fruit: banana, apple, mango, papaya, chikoo, melon
- Soaked & chopped almonds, walnuts *(no whole nuts for under-4 — choking risk)*
- Roasted makhana (fox nuts) with a little ghee
- Homemade laddoo: ragi, besan, dates, or sesame
- Steamed dhokla or idli
- Curd / yogurt or buttermilk
- Cheese cubes or paneer cubes
- Boiled corn or sweet potato
- Sprouts chaat (mild) or moong salad
- Banana/mango milkshake or fruit smoothie
- Peanut or sesame chikki (small pieces)
- Murmura / poha chivda (lightly roasted)
- Vegetable sticks (cucumber, carrot) with hung-curd dip

## 5. Healthy eating tips 🥗

- Eat together as a family — kids copy what they see.
- Never force-feed. Offer; let the child decide *how much*.
- Offer variety and re-offer new foods many times (it can take 10+ tries).
- Limit sugar, fried, and packaged snacks; keep treats occasional.
- Include iron-rich foods: ragi, jaggery, green leafy veg, dal, eggs.
- Add protein + dairy daily: dal, paneer, curd, milk, eggs, beans.
- Hydrate with water and milk — not juice, soda, or sugary drinks.
- Cut food small and supervise meals (watch grapes, nuts, hard pieces — choking).
- Involve kids in simple cooking — they eat what they help make.
- Small frequent meals (3 meals + 2 snacks) suit small tummies.

## 6. Daily health habits 💚

- **Sleep:** ~11–14 hrs/day for 2–3 yrs and ~10–13 hrs for 3–6 yrs (including naps).
- **Active play:** at least 3 hours of movement spread across the day.
- **Screen time:** AAP suggests under 1 hour/day of quality content for ages 2–5, with a
  grown-up alongside. (This app is designed for short, shared sessions.)
- **Check-ups & vaccines:** keep regular pediatric visits and immunizations on schedule.
- **Teeth:** brush twice daily; first dental visit by age 1–2.
- **Sun & nutrients:** balanced diet + sunlight; ask your doctor about vitamin D / iron.
- **Hygiene:** wash hands before meals and after play.
- **Connection:** cuddles, reading, and talking fuel the developing brain.
